FIGURE CAPTION
A controlled learning-content architecture connects a five-level learning-object hierarchy, nine core content entities, six metadata categories, version control, a nine-stage content lifecycle, six ownership roles and seven integrity controls to one traceable path from learner need to readiness decision.

ALT TEXT
A square systems infographic titled “Learning Content Architecture” explains how controlled learning material supports competencies, assessments and readiness decisions without becoming a curriculum or technology design. The header asks how learning content should be organized so every competency, assessment and readiness decision is supported by controlled, complete and traceable material, and limits the scope to standard technical work. A five-level hierarchy runs from Learning Content Architecture to Content collection, Learning object, Content component and Presentation asset, with Learning object emphasized as the primary control unit. A nine-item core-entity panel defines Content collection, Learning object, Content component, Presentation asset, Controlled reference, Relationship record, Version record, Review and approval record, and Archive record. A traceability chain connects Learner-journey need, Competency behaviour, Learning object, Assessment method, Evidence category and Readiness decision, with the statement that learning content supports preparation and does not prove competence. Six metadata categories cover identity and classification, learning and competency alignment, assessment and evidence alignment, technical authority, control and lifecycle, and publication and confidentiality. Eight version-control principles address persistent identity, one active released version, no silent overwrite, change classification, impact assessment, supersession, the distinction between version and approval, and asset consistency. A nine-stage lifecycle moves from need identification through scoping, drafting, learning-quality review, technical review, architecture and traceability review, approval and release, monitored use, and revision, supersession, retirement or archive; a controlled revision loop returns changes to the earlier stages. A six-role ownership strip assigns reconstructed responsibilities to the New Engineer, Technical Trainer, Senior Specialist, Engineering Manager, Learning or Competency Lead and Project-Delivery Manager. Seven coverage and integrity controls test for orphan objects, competency gaps, assessment-support gaps, duplicate content, conflicts, stale links and version misalignment. The final split panel contrasts the current-state diagnosis that learning material is fragmented, inconsistently controlled and not traceable to one competency and readiness logic with reconstructed architecture logic based on controlled learning objects, explicit relationships and versioned approval.
