TITLE
Competency Assessment Framework for Independent Standard Work

BUSINESS QUESTION
How should competence be assessed so that independent standard work can be approved consistently using observable evidence rather than informal judgment?

PURPOSE
Define how representative performance is observed, evidenced, reviewed and converted into consistent competency and readiness decisions.

SCOPE
STANDARD TECHNICAL WORK ONLY

ASSESSMENT PRINCIPLES

Evidence-based
Decisions use recorded evidence, not confidence or attendance.

Representative performance
Activities reflect the standard work the learner is expected to perform.

Observable behaviour
Behaviours must be seen, reviewed or otherwise verified.

Repeatability
Evidence must show that required performance can be reproduced.

Reliability
Comparable evidence should support comparable conclusions.

Consistency
Equivalent cases use equivalent expectations and decision rules.

Validity
Each method assesses the capability it claims to measure.

Proportionality
Evidence burden matches task complexity, uncertainty and risk.

Standardization
Common criteria, records and decision rules control assessment.

Transparency
Expectations, evidence and decisions are understandable to relevant stakeholders.

Authenticity
Evidence must reflect the learner's own performance and decisions.

Development and judgment separated
Support is recorded and does not inflate the demonstrated level.

ASSESSMENT METHODS

Methods are combined according to the competency domain, performance level and evidence required.

Direct observation
Purpose: Observe sequence, decisions, checks, support and escalation.
Primary use: Workflow execution · Judgment · Task ownership

Representative work task
Purpose: Demonstrate integrated performance on realistic standard work.
Primary use: All six competency domains

Technical discussion
Purpose: Confirm knowledge, rationale, boundaries and result meaning.
Primary use: Knowledge · Judgment · Interpretation and quality

Documentation review
Purpose: Check inputs, assumptions, decisions, outputs and traceability.
Primary use: Documentation and traceability

Result-interpretation review
Purpose: Explain principal results and identify evident anomalies.
Primary use: Interpretation and quality · Judgment

Quality-check review
Purpose: Verify required checks and response to findings.
Primary use: Interpretation and quality · Workflow execution

Scenario discussion
Purpose: Examine response to uncertainty, abnormal results and scope boundaries.
Primary use: Judgment · Task ownership

Decision explanation
Purpose: Distinguish deliberate choices from imitation or guessing.
Primary use: Knowledge · Judgment

Escalation discussion
Purpose: Test recognition and communication of genuine exceptions.
Primary use: Judgment · Task ownership

Support-history review
Purpose: Distinguish guided, limited-support and independent performance.
Primary use: All performance-based domains

ASSESSMENT EVIDENCE

No single evidence type establishes readiness.

Knowledge evidence
Explanations, sequencing, terminology and required-check recognition.
Examples: Workflow explanation · terminology use · required-check identification

Performance evidence
Observed execution and completed representative standard work.
Examples: Observed task · completed output · corrected workflow steps

Judgment evidence
Decision rationale, uncertainty recognition and scope-boundary choices.
Examples: Decision record · identified uncertainty · justified escalation

Interpretation and quality evidence
Result explanation, required checks and evident anomaly recognition.
Examples: Result interpretation · quality-check record · identified anomaly

Documentation evidence
Inputs, assumptions, decisions, outputs and review history.
Examples: Work record · file and version status · review response

Autonomy and escalation evidence
Support required, routine issues resolved and genuine exceptions escalated.
Examples: Support record · task without routine intervention · correct escalation
Sub-elements: Independence · Support · Escalation

ASSESSMENT WORKFLOW

1. Assessment planned
2. Representative task assigned
3. Performance observed
4. Evidence collected
5. Evidence reviewed
6. Competency levels assigned
7. Development needs identified
8. Readiness decision made
9. Decision recorded and communicated

REASSESSMENT LOOP
Targeted development → additional evidence → reassessment

DECISION LOGIC

Competency level decision
Assign the highest level fully supported by evidence in each domain.

Development decision
Identify the unmet behaviour, missing evidence and reassessment need.

Readiness decision
Approve only when integrated Level 4 performance is demonstrated across all required domains.

Insufficient or conflicting evidence → Not yet demonstrated or further review required.

Judgment, quality control, documentation and escalation barriers cannot be averaged away.

Exact scoring and weighting belong to the later assessment system.

ASSESSMENT RESPONSIBILITIES

New Engineer
Would complete representative work, explain decisions and disclose support received.

Technical Trainer
Would observe workflow performance, record support and provide structured feedback.

Senior Specialist
Would validate technical judgment, interpretation and scope-boundary decisions.

Engineering Manager
Would define standard-work scope and make the readiness decision from available evidence.

Learning or Competency Lead
Would support consistent evidence review and assessor interpretation where involved.

Project-Delivery Manager
Would use the approved readiness status when allocating standard work.

CURRENT-STATE DIAGNOSIS
Independent-work readiness cannot be verified consistently.

RECONSTRUCTED ASSESSMENT LOGIC
Observable evidence, structured review and integrated domain decisions would provide a consistent basis for approving independent standard work.

QUALIFIER
Reconstructed framework logic; not a claim of historical implementation.
