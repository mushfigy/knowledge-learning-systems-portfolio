TITLE
Competency Framework for Independent Standard Work

CONTEXT
The framework defines the observable progression and evidence required before a new engineer can be approved for independent standard technical work.

SCOPE
STANDARD TECHNICAL WORK ONLY

FRAMEWORK PURPOSE
Define what a learner must know, perform and demonstrate at increasing levels of support.

COMPETENCY DOMAINS

1. Software and workflow knowledge
Explains the software purpose, terminology, required inputs, outputs and standard workflow structure.

2. Standard workflow execution
Completes the approved standard workflow accurately, in sequence and with usable outputs.

3. Technical judgment and boundary recognition
Makes routine technical decisions, explains rationale and recognizes when work exceeds the standard scope.

4. Result interpretation and quality control
Interprets principal results, performs required checks and identifies evident anomalies before review.

5. Documentation and traceability
Records inputs, assumptions, decisions, outputs and review history in a traceable form.

6. Task ownership and escalation
Plans assigned standard work, manages routine uncertainty, incorporates feedback and escalates genuine exceptions.

COMPETENCY LEVELS

Level 1 — Knows
Definition:
Explains the purpose, terminology, sequence and basic decision logic of standard work.

Support condition:
Explanation, observation and frequent prompting.

Expected observable performance:
Describes what must be done, why it is required and where guidance is needed.

Evidence condition:
Supported by explanation, sequencing and recognition evidence.

Limitation:
Does not establish reliable task performance.

Level 2 — Performs with guidance
Definition:
Completes defined parts of standard work with step-by-step direction and correction.

Support condition:
Active instruction, correction and confirmation of routine decisions.

Expected observable performance:
Reproduces assigned workflow steps and applies corrections during the task.

Evidence condition:
Supported by observed guided performance and records of assistance provided.

Limitation:
Does not establish readiness for work without active guidance.

Level 3 — Performs with limited support
Definition:
Completes standard work with limited prompting and requests support for specific uncertainties.

Support condition:
Targeted clarification or review rather than continuous instruction.

Expected observable performance:
Performs most of the task, completes routine checks and identifies justified support needs.

Evidence condition:
Supported by representative performance with documented episodic assistance.

Limitation:
Does not yet establish repeatable independent performance across all required domains.

Level 4 — Performs independently
Definition:
Completes standard technical work consistently, verifies outputs and escalates exceptions without routine supervision.

Support condition:
No routine prompting; support is requested only for justified exceptions or scope boundaries.

Expected observable performance:
Integrates knowledge, execution, judgment, quality control, documentation and task ownership.

Evidence condition:
Supported by repeatable representative performance and documented support and escalation behaviour.

Limitation:
Independent performance applies only within defined standard technical work. It does not grant specialist authority or unrestricted approval authority.

COMPLETE 24-CELL COMPETENCY MATRIX

Domain 1 — Software and workflow knowledge
Level 1 — Knows:
Explains the software purpose, terminology, inputs, outputs and standard workflow sequence.

Level 2 — Performs with guidance:
Identifies required steps, inputs and outputs with trainer guidance for a standard task.

Level 3 — Performs with limited support:
Explains workflow choices, required inputs, outputs and checks with targeted clarification.

Level 4 — Performs independently:
Selects and justifies required workflow steps, inputs, outputs and checks without routine prompting.

Domain 2 — Standard workflow execution
Level 1 — Knows:
Sequences the main workflow steps and identifies the expected output.

Level 2 — Performs with guidance:
Completes defined workflow steps accurately with step-by-step direction and correction.

Level 3 — Performs with limited support:
Completes the standard workflow with limited prompting and corrects identified errors.

Level 4 — Performs independently:
Completes the full standard workflow accurately, consistently and without routine intervention.

Domain 3 — Technical judgment and boundary recognition
Level 1 — Knows:
Identifies routine decisions, known limits and situations requiring escalation.

Level 2 — Performs with guidance:
Makes routine choices after confirmation and escalates uncertainties when directed.

Level 3 — Performs with limited support:
Makes routine decisions, explains rationale and escalates specific uncertainties appropriately.

Level 4 — Performs independently:
Makes justified routine decisions and escalates work beyond the approved standard scope.

Domain 4 — Result interpretation and quality control
Level 1 — Knows:
Describes expected result patterns and identifies the required quality checks.

Level 2 — Performs with guidance:
Performs prescribed checks with guidance and recognizes issues after prompting.

Level 3 — Performs with limited support:
Interprets principal results, performs standard checks and identifies evident anomalies.

Level 4 — Performs independently:
Verifies inputs and outputs, interprets results and identifies anomalies without routine prompting.

Domain 5 — Documentation and traceability
Level 1 — Knows:
Identifies required records, assumptions, file status and review information.

Level 2 — Performs with guidance:
Documents assigned steps and updates records after prompted correction.

Level 3 — Performs with limited support:
Records inputs, assumptions, decisions and results with limited correction.

Level 4 — Performs independently:
Produces complete, traceable documentation and responds effectively to review comments.

Domain 6 — Task ownership and escalation
Level 1 — Knows:
Describes task responsibilities, support routes and limits of the assigned standard scope.

Level 2 — Performs with guidance:
Completes assigned portions, incorporates feedback and requests support when prompted.

Level 3 — Performs with limited support:
Plans most task activities, manages routine uncertainty and requests specific support.

Level 4 — Performs independently:
Owns the standard task, incorporates feedback and escalates genuine exceptions appropriately.

EVIDENCE BAND

Knowledge evidence
Shows what the learner can explain, sequence and recognize.
Examples: workflow explanation; terminology use; required-check identification.
Relationship: primarily Domain 1; supports Domains 3 and 4.

Performance evidence
Shows execution of the standard workflow in representative work.
Examples: observed task; completed output; corrected workflow steps.
Relationship: primarily Domain 2; supports Domain 6.

Judgment evidence
Shows decision rationale, boundary recognition and appropriate escalation.
Examples: decision record; identified uncertainty; correctly escalated exception.
Relationship: primarily Domain 3; supports Domain 6.

Interpretation and quality evidence
Shows that results were interpreted and checked before review.
Examples: result explanation; quality-check record; identified anomaly.
Relationship: primarily Domain 4; supports Domain 3.

Documentation evidence
Shows that inputs, decisions, outputs and review history are traceable.
Examples: work record; file and version identification; review response.
Relationship: primarily Domain 5; supports all domains.

Autonomy and escalation evidence
Shows the support required and whether routine issues were handled appropriately.
Examples: support record; task without routine intervention; correctly escalated exception.
Relationship: primarily Domain 6; distinguishes Levels 2–4 across all domains.

No single evidence type establishes readiness.

READINESS-DECISION LOGIC

1. Demonstrated level by domain
Assign the highest level supported by observable evidence in each competency domain.

2. Development need
Identify the unmet behaviour, affected domain and further practice or support required.

3. Independent-work readiness
Approve independent standard work only when integrated Level 4 performance is demonstrated across all required domains.

Judgment, quality control, documentation and escalation barriers cannot be averaged away.

Exact scoring and weighting belong to the later assessment system.

STAKEHOLDER RESPONSIBILITIES

New Engineer
Would perform representative work, explain decisions and retain required evidence.

Technical Trainer
Would observe workflow performance, record support and provide structured feedback.

Senior Specialist
Would validate technical judgment, interpretation and recognition of scope boundaries.

Engineering Manager
Would define standard-work scope and make the readiness decision from available evidence.

Learning or Competency Lead
Would support consistent interpretation and consolidate evidence where involved.

Project-Delivery Manager
Would use the approved readiness status when allocating standard work.

CURRENT-STATE DIAGNOSIS
Independent-work readiness cannot be verified consistently.

FRAMEWORK-PURPOSE STATEMENT
Independent standard work may be approved only when integrated performance is demonstrated across all required domains.
