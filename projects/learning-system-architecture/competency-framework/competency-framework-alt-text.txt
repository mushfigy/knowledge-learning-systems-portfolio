FIGURE CAPTION
A six-domain, four-level competency matrix defines observable progression from knowledge to independent standard performance, supported by six evidence categories and integrated readiness logic that applies only to defined standard technical work.

ALT TEXT
A competency framework titled “Competency Framework for Independent Standard Work” is presented as a six-row by four-column matrix. The six competency domains are Software and workflow knowledge; Standard workflow execution; Technical judgment and boundary recognition; Result interpretation and quality control; Documentation and traceability; and Task ownership and escalation. The four columns show observable progression from Level 1, Knows, to Level 2, Performs with guidance, Level 3, Performs with limited support, and Level 4, Performs independently. Each of the 24 cells contains one observable performance statement. The Level 4 header states that the scope is standard work only and does not confer specialist authority. Beneath the matrix, an evidence band contains six categories: knowledge, performance, judgment, interpretation and quality, documentation, and autonomy and escalation evidence. It states that no single evidence type establishes readiness. A readiness-decision panel separates demonstrated level by domain, development need and independent-work readiness, and states that judgment, quality control, documentation and escalation barriers cannot be averaged away. A six-role stakeholder strip describes reconstructed responsibilities for the New Engineer, Technical Trainer, Senior Specialist, Engineering Manager, Learning or Competency Lead and Project-Delivery Manager. The final split panel contrasts the current-state diagnosis, “Independent-work readiness cannot be verified consistently,” with the intended framework logic that independent standard work may be approved only when integrated performance is demonstrated across all required domains.
