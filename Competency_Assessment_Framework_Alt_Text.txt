FIGURE CAPTION
A structured competency-assessment framework combines assessment principles, complementary methods, six evidence categories, evidence-quality controls, a nine-stage workflow and integrated decision logic to support consistent approval of independent standard technical work.

ALT TEXT
A tall systems infographic titled “Competency Assessment Framework for Independent Standard Work” explains how observable evidence would replace informal readiness judgment. The header states that the scope is standard technical work only and asks how competence should be assessed so that independent work can be approved consistently. A twelve-item assessment-principles panel covers evidence-based assessment, representative performance, observable behaviour, repeatability, reliability, consistency, validity, proportionality, standardization, transparency, authenticity and separation of development support from assessment judgment. A ten-method panel includes direct observation, representative work tasks, technical discussion, documentation review, result-interpretation review, quality-check review, scenario discussion, decision explanation, escalation discussion and support-history review. A six-section evidence band retains the categories Knowledge evidence, Performance evidence, Judgment evidence, Interpretation and quality evidence, Documentation evidence, and Autonomy and escalation evidence; independence, support and escalation appear only as sub-elements of the final category. A separate evidence-quality panel lists representative, current, observable, repeatable, traceable, authentic, sufficient, relevant and consistent. A two-row nine-stage workflow moves from assessment planning through task assignment, observation, evidence collection and review, competency-level assignment, development decisions, readiness decisions and recorded communication. A reassessment loop returns development needs to a representative task through targeted development and additional evidence. A decision panel separates competency-level, development and readiness decisions and states that critical weaknesses cannot be averaged away. Six stakeholder sections describe reconstructed responsibilities for the New Engineer, Technical Trainer, Senior Specialist, Engineering Manager, Learning or Competency Lead and Project-Delivery Manager. The final split panel contrasts the current-state diagnosis, “Independent-work readiness cannot be verified consistently,” with reconstructed assessment logic based on observable evidence, structured review and integrated domain decisions.
